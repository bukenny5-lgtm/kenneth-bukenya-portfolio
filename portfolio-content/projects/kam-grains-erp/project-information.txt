PROJECT OVERVIEW
KAM GRAINS ERP is a full-stack business management system built for a grain trading operation. It integrates procurement, goods receiving, lot-based inventory, cleaning and reclassification, sales orders, deliveries, Quick Sale, customer returns, accounts receivable, accounts payable, payment accounts, till operations, finance, reporting and audit controls.

The system connects browser workflows to a REST API and PostgreSQL business schemas. Operational documents can create controlled stock movements and financial journal entries, allowing users to follow a transaction from its source document through inventory, receivables or payables, payment and reporting. Delivery is treated as the primary sales-reporting event, while customer returns preserve traceable inventory and financial reversals.

The implementation also includes role- and permission-aware navigation, branch/location context, backdated transaction controls, Excel exports, weekly reporting, customer analytics and development foundations for EFRIS compliance and till-session management. The project is actively developed and operationally implemented; features that are not production-approved are identified as such rather than presented as finished integrations.

BUSINESS CHALLENGE
Previously disconnected operational activities made it difficult to maintain one reliable view of purchasing, stock, sales, customer balances, supplier balances and financial postings. The business needed a system that reflected real grain-trading workflows, including lot-level stock, goods received against purchasing, cleaning outputs, delivery-based sales recognition, payment controls and auditability.

The system also needed to support controlled corrections and backdated transactions without losing the original document or posting history. Reporting had to use business transaction dates and consistent weekly periods, while customer analysis needed to cover performance, dormancy and RFM-style segmentation.

SOLUTION
A modular React and Node.js ERP was implemented over a PostgreSQL database with domain-specific schemas and SQL posting functions. The frontend exposes permission-gated operational pages and reporting views. The backend provides REST routes, authentication middleware, location/branch access controls, workflow services and database-backed posting boundaries.

Core business events are represented explicitly: purchase receipts create inventory records, deliveries create SALE_ISSUE movements, customer returns create return movements and related financial adjustments, and financial transactions create controlled journal entries. Reporting views combine operational events using verified business rules, and audit records preserve transaction and backdate traceability.

BUSINESS WORKFLOWS
Procure-to-pay follows supplier, purchase order, status/approval control, goods received note, purchase receipt and lot/stock update, AP invoice, AP payment, payment account, journal, reconciliation, reporting and audit.

Order-to-cash follows customer, sales order, delivery, SALE_ISSUE inventory posting, lot/cost determination, financial posting, AR invoice, customer receipt, payment account or till, reconciliation and management reporting. Quick Sale provides a direct POS branch, including inventory and payment/till integration. Customer returns create traceable inventory and financial reversal or settlement effects according to their source, disposition and posting state.

Inventory movement workflows cover purchase receipts, product-lot creation, stock-on-hand changes, cleaning/reclassification, internal requests, inter-site transfer dispatch and receipt, delivery issue, Quick Sale issue, customer return, stock count, stock adjustment, the movement ledger, audit history and inventory reporting.

DATABASE ARCHITECTURE
The PostgreSQL design separates business concerns into verified schemas:

- app — company, branch, location, payment and operational configuration
- sec — users, roles, permissions and access mappings
- pur — procurement, goods receipts, supplier invoices and payments
- inv — products, lots, stock balances, movement headers and movement lines
- sal — sales orders, deliveries, POS sales, AR, returns and sales events
- fin — chart of accounts, journals and journal lines
- audit — audit records, including backdated transaction events
- reporting — reporting views and analytics, including unified sales-event reporting

SQL functions, triggers and migrations enforce or support posting, balance checks, branch attribution, return settlement, backdate logging, inventory movement creation and reporting logic.

REPORTING AND ANALYTICS
Reporting uses transaction_date and treats delivery as the primary sales business event rather than the sales order. Weekly operational reports use Monday-to-Sunday periods. Verified reporting areas include management summaries, sales by product, purchases by product, profit by product, customer weekly performance, dormant customers, customer concentration and RFM-related customer analysis. Inventory reports expose stock and movement information, while Excel export support provides operational file outputs.

IMPORTANT TECHNICAL DECISIONS
- Use delivery as the sales-reporting event while retaining sales orders as pre-delivery documents.
- Represent stock changes with movement headers and lines rather than opaque balance-only updates.
- Preserve product lots and source movement costs to support traceability and FIFO-related cost determination.
- Keep financial posting in controlled SQL/database boundaries with balanced journal assertions.
- Use feature flags and business profiles for capabilities that are not universally enabled.
- Keep EFRIS as a separate, readiness-gated compliance foundation rather than inventing an unverified URA transport.
- Preserve original documents and create reversal or settlement records instead of destructive edits.
- Use Monday-to-Sunday reporting periods and transaction dates to match business operations.
